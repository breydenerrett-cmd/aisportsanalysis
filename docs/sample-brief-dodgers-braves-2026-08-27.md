**The read:** Sale's 5.65 career mark against Los Angeles is mostly White
Sox- and Red Sox-era noise and shouldn't move you much. What should: he has
gone exactly six innings in each of his last four starts, so this is a
6-inning start whatever happens, and Atlanta's pen has to cover the ninth
either way. Yamamoto is the rarer bird — a road-better arm, a full run of
ERA cleaner away from Chavez Ravine, into a lineup he has never allowed a
run to.

## The Card

1. **First 5 Innings — Under 4.5** — target −120 or better · 1.5u · **Best
   bet**
   The cleanest expression of what's actually true here. You get six
   innings of Sale at his 1.65 home ERA and six of Yamamoto at his 1.99 road
   ERA, against two lineups that are league-average at best in tonight's
   handedness split — and you get out before either bullpen touches the
   ball. Every reason to doubt these teams (tired pens, blown saves,
   walk-offs) happens after this bet has already graded. If the F5 line is
   only 4.0, take the under to −115 and no worse.

2. **Full Game — Over 6.5** — shop for 6.5, not 7 · 1u · **Play**
   Not a contradiction with bet #1 — the other half of the same thesis. The
   market has stapled two aces together and printed the lowest total on the
   board, then ignored that Atlanta has four healthy relievers and Los
   Angeles is handing late innings to a closer who has lost the game on
   consecutive nights. Six quiet innings and then three loud ones gets you
   to 7+ comfortably. Only fire this at 6.5. At 7 the price is gone; pass
   and keep bet #1.

3. **Dodgers Moneyline** — +100 or better only · 1u · **Value, thin**
   The better team, the better offense, and the pitcher with the friendlier
   split is priced as a small underdog because the Braves just won two in a
   row. Los Angeles is 7–3 in its last ten with a +150 run differential;
   Atlanta is 4–6 and fading. Genuine edge but a small one — the models
   split 51/49 in both directions. Caesars was showing +101; if you can't
   get plus money, skip it rather than lay −110. On Polymarket, the same
   bet is any Dodgers contract at 50¢ or below.

4. **Chris Sale Over 6.5 Strikeouts** — to −130 · 0.5u · **Lean**
   11.07 K/9 across a six-inning start projects to roughly 7.4 punchouts,
   and he has 32 in his last four outings. The catch is the hard
   six-inning ceiling — he isn't getting a seventh to bail you out, so a
   slow first two innings kills it. Half a unit, and only if the line is
   6.5. At 7.5 it's a pass.

5. **Run line, either side** — **Pass**
   Dodgers −1.5 at −200 is a terrible price in a game projected to be
   decided by one or two runs, and Braves +1.5 at −215 is worse. Also
   passing on Yamamoto strikeouts — 6.3 per start is a low base and the
   number is usually set above where the model wants it.

## What Would Make This Wrong

- **The lineup card.** It isn't out yet — posts a few hours before first
  pitch. Getaway-day finale after two late losses, so Dave Roberts is a
  real candidate to rest Betts, Freeman, or Muncy. If two regulars sit, the
  Dodgers moneyline is dead and the F5 under gets even stronger.
- **A rain delay.** A 35% storm chance is a coin-flip-adjacent risk of
  losing a starter early. That single event flips the under from a good bet
  to a bad one and hands the over a free win. If storms are actively
  rolling through at 6 PM ET, don't fire bet #1.
- **Sale's control.** He walks 28 men in 135 innings. If he's missing early
  against a patient Dodgers lineup, the pitch count eats the sixth inning
  and the whole "aces go deep" premise collapses.
- **The models disagree with each other.** SportsGrid has Atlanta at 56%
  and likes the over 7; Covers has Los Angeles at 53% and flags over 6.5 as
  the value. That spread is the honest signal here — the sides are close to
  a coin flip and the total is where the actual edge sits.
