# Placing bets for customers, and a community pool: assessment

Written 2026-09-15 in reply to the owner's message that day ("There's not a way
to place bets for people in individual accounts that they fund? Or a way to let
them take a slice by contributing in the community fund ... like a community
pool"). A plain-English reading of same-day research, **not legal advice**,
corrected after an adversarial review by a second reader acting as gaming and
securities counsel. **[secondary]** marks trade press, law-firm commentary or
search summaries; unmarked links are primary sources. Research read date:
2026-09-15.

## 1. The short answer

1. **No lawful way was found for LINEHOUND as it exists to place bets with
   customers' money or to run a real-money pool.** The only lawful models found
   are becoming a licensed sportsbook or exchange in each state, or a
   registered securities or commodities offering, and even registration would
   not make bets placed at sportsbooks lawful.
2. **Both would expose the business and the people running it to criminal
   charges, not only fines**: state bookmaking and pool-selling offences,
   Arizona's agent-betting offence, and the federal illegal-gambling-business,
   unlicensed-money-transmission and money-laundering charges seen in the
   cases below.
3. **Promoting either one is itself a risk.** Advertising a fund, even a
   waitlist, can be an unregistered securities offer, and LINEHOUND's own
   testing found no proven edge in MLB (0 of 8,811 strategies survived; the
   MLB picks show no positive estimated return under the market benchmark;
   internal record, [EVOLAB_PHASE2B_RESULTS.md](EVOLAB_PHASE2B_RESULTS.md)).
   A pool would be sold on a record that does not exist. **Do not mention a
   fund, managed accounts or expected profit publicly.**

Lower-risk models that competitors already use, each subject to counsel review:
selling each sport separately, a free paper leaderboard, and letting members act
on picks in their own accounts (section 4).

## 2. Placing bets for customers

**What regulators say.** In the eight states reviewed, a bet placed as someone's
agent or proxy is a bet licensed sportsbooks must refuse:

- **New Jersey** lists "any individual placing a wager as an agent or proxy" as
  a prohibited participant ([N.J.A.C. 13:69N-1.1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-13-69N-1-1)
  [secondary: confirmed by search, not the official PDF]).
- **Colorado** ([rules](https://sbg.colorado.gov/sites/sbg/files/documents/SBETRules1%2C6%2C9RedlineCommission.pdf))
  and **Michigan** ([rules](https://www.michigan.gov/-/media/Project/Websites/mgcb/Internet-Gaming-and-Fantasy-Contests/ActsandRules/Internet_Sports_Betting_Draft_Rules_2020-8-26doc.pdf))
  use similar language [secondary: search summaries; the links are a commission
  redline and a 2020 draft, not the adopted rules]. Colorado carves out approved
  contests and weekly picks.
- **Illinois** bars wagering for a third party only where that person is
  already prohibited ([rule](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/illinois/Ill-Admin-Code-tit-11-SS-1900.1120)).
- **Pennsylvania** requires sportsbooks' account terms to forbid anyone else
  using an account ([rules index](https://regulations.justia.com/states/pennsylvania/title-58/part-vii/subpart-q/chapter-1408a)
  [secondary: section text reconstructed from search]).
- **New York**'s existing mobile-wagering rules already require sportsbooks to
  refuse prohibited bettors and monitor for proxy betting [secondary: search
  summary]. A standalone ban ([A9584-A](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/A9584/amendment/A))
  passed the Assembly 142-0 in March 2026 and was in Senate committee on the
  read date.
- **Ohio:** nothing found, which is not permission.

Most of these are duties placed on sportsbooks. **Arizona is different in
kind:** its criminal code makes it a class 1 misdemeanour for anyone to
"knowingly accept for a fee" anything of value "to be transmitted or delivered
for wagering or betting" ([A.R.S. 13-3305](https://codes.findlaw.com/az/title-13-criminal-code/az-rev-st-sect-13-3305.html)),
which describes the service itself. Its exclusions and neighbouring offences
were not researched.

**Criminal gambling statutes reach this directly too.** Texas defines
bookmaking to include forwarding more than five bets, or more than $1,000 in
bets, in 24 hours ([47.01](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/penal-code/penal-sect-47-01/),
[47.03](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/penal-code/penal-sect-47-03/)). California
Penal Code 337a separately criminalises receiving, holding or forwarding money
to be wagered; repeat offences can be felonies ([summary](https://www.losangelescriminallawyer.pro/california-penal-code-section-337a-pc-pool-selling-bookmaking-wa.html)
[secondary: defence-firm summary; statute not read]).

**Federal exposure.** Taking money to bet could make LINEHOUND "engaged in the
business of betting or wagering", which triggers the Wire Act
([18 U.S.C. 1084](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1084)). If that
happens, the Wire Act also reaches transmitting information assisting in placing
bets, so **the picks themselves could become exposed** [reviewer reading; verify
with counsel, including the 1084(b) safe harbour]. UIGEA bars accepting payments
tied to internet bets that are unlawful under state law
([5363](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5363)). If the service breaks
state gambling law (most clearly the bookmaking statutes or Arizona's), and five
or more people run, finance, manage or own it, and it operates more than 30 days
or takes $2,000 in gross revenue in a day, it meets the federal "illegal gambling
business" definition ([18 U.S.C. 1955](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1955)).
Holding customer money to move it risks unlicensed money transmission
([31 CFR 1010.100(ff)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1010/subpart-A/section-1010.100);
criminal charge [18 U.S.C. 1960](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title18-section1960)).
Money-laundering statutes, forfeiture and federal wagering taxes were not
researched.

**Sportsbook terms.** DraftKings bars proxy wagering and allows one account per
person ([terms](https://sportsbook.draftkings.com/legal/watl-terms-of-use)
[secondary: search summary; check the live page]). FanDuel's 2022 fantasy
(Skill Gaming) terms forbid letting "anyone else access or use your account" and
commercial use without authorisation ([terms](https://s3.amazonaws.com/cdn.fanduel.com/images/2022/terms/20220315_Skill+Gaming+Terms+Conditions_FINAL.pdf));
its current sportsbook terms were not read. In practice books can void such bets
and withhold balances, leaving LINEHOUND answerable to customers whose money was
lost.

**Enforcement found.** New Jersey fined DraftKings $150,000 for failing to stop
a Florida bettor routing $3M+ through a proxy ([Sports Handle](https://sportshandle.com/draftkings-proxy-betting-settlement-new-jersey-fine/)
[secondary]); the fine was against the operator, and the research did not find
what happened to the proxy. DOJ sentenced Jason Feinman to 27 months for an
illegal gambling business, money laundering and tax evasion
([DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/california-man-sentenced-prison-evading-taxes-more-4m-million-income-and-operating-illegal)).
Mathew Bowyer pleaded guilty to operating an unlawful gambling business, money
laundering and filing a false tax return ([iGaming Business](https://igamingbusiness.com/sports-betting/bowyer-illegal-bookmaking-plea/)
[secondary]). None involved a picks service.

**Lawful version.** The only lawful model found is becoming a licensed
sportsbook or exchange in each state, as Sporttrade did in Colorado
([Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/colorado-sporttrade-sports-betting-license-approve-august-2023)
[secondary]). No licence was found that lets a third party bet customers' money
at another operator's sportsbook.

## 3. A community pool

**Securities.** Members pay in, LINEHOUND chooses the bets, results are shared:
under the *Howey* test that is likely an investment contract (research reading;
[Burr & Forman](https://www.burr.com/securities-litigation/color-gambling-green-staking-security)
[secondary] covers staking an individual gambler, an analogy only). A pool
resembles two 2019 SEC cases against sports-betting funds: Thomas and Becker's
"sports club" LLCs, $29M from 600+ investors promised 250-600% from a
"proprietary handicapping system" ([LR-24585](https://www.sec.gov/enforcement-litigation/litigation-releases/lr-24585)),
and Bettor Investments, about $145,500 from about 70 investors
([LR-24547](https://www.sec.gov/enforcement-litigation/litigation-releases/lr-24547)).
Both were brought mainly as frauds and each also charged an unregistered
offering; releases are allegations, not rulings. Whether an honest pool would
face the registration charge alone is an inference, not a holding. The owner's
own example ($100 a month on $1,000, about 10% a month) is a projected return of
the kind behind those fraud charges. State securities laws were not researched.

**State gambling law.** California (receiving, holding or forwarding money to be
wagered, per the secondary summary above) and Texas (forwarding bets) describe a
pool's conduct directly. Florida's bookmaking felony
([849.25](https://law.justia.com/codes/florida/2023/title-xlvi/chapter-849/section-849-25/),
2023 edition) and New York's definition ([225.00](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/PEN/225.00))
centre on taking bets, so their reach would turn on how a prosecutor
characterises the pool.

**Money.** Holding deposits and paying withdrawals without a licence risks the
federal unlicensed money-transmitting charge (18 U.S.C. 1960, above); FinCEN has
said gambling operations outside its casino rules can be money transmitters
([WSGR](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/how-gaming-companies-can-become-inadvertent-money-services-businesses.html)
[secondary]).

**The event-contract route.** Kalshi, a CFTC-registered exchange, offers sports
contracts ([Kalshi, describing itself](https://kalshi.com/market-integrity/regulation)).
The Third Circuit (New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Delaware) held 2-1 in April 2026
that federal law preempts New Jersey's gambling law for Kalshi's exchange
([opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca3/25-1922/25-1922-2026-04-06.html));
it did not address pools, and courts elsewhere have gone the other way at the
preliminary stage. The CFTC and DOJ sued Arizona, Connecticut and Illinois
([Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/cftc-sues-illinois-arizona-connecticut-prediction-markets-april-2-2026)
[secondary]); Connecticut sued Kalshi in August 2026
([CT AG](https://portal.ct.gov/ag/press-releases/2026-press-releases/connecticut-sues-kalshi-to-stop-illegal-unlicensed-sports-betting));
Washington ordered Kalshi to stop ([Tech Times](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/324682/20260817/washington-bans-kalshi-sports-contracts-financial-data-markets-survive-court-test.htm)
[secondary]). A pool trading those contracts for others would need commodity
pool operator registration, or an exemption limited to qualified investors and
small trading volumes ([17 CFR 4.13](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-I/part-4/subpart-A/section-4.13);
[CFTC](https://www.cftc.gov/sites/default/files/tm/tmcpo_cta_exemptions.htm)),
on top of contracts whose legality is unsettled.

**Lawful version.** Securities or commodities registration would not cure the
gambling problem: a pool betting at sportsbooks remains unlicensed bookmaking or
proxy betting under the state laws above. No compliant open pool was found.

## 4. Lower-risk alternatives others already use (each subject to counsel review)

1. **Per-sport subscriptions.** Sharp Football prices NFL and college football
   separately plus a bundle ([Sharp](https://www.sharpfootballanalysis.com/betting-packages/));
   Establish The Run sells single-sport and all-sport tiers
   ([FantasyLabs](https://www.fantasylabs.com/pricing-etr/)). No industry norm
   was found for pricing as a share of the service's own profit. On the MLB
   record, "5 to 20% of expected profit" prices at or near zero, and quoting an
   expected profit is an earnings claim that needs substantiation under FTC Act
   section 5. Plan: [PER_SPORT_PRICING_PLAN.md](PER_SPORT_PRICING_PLAN.md).
2. **Free paper leaderboard.** Delivers the shared-progress experience and a
   simulated record. Avoid the word "fund". Label results as simulated and
   never present them later as real returns. Prizes need a free way to enter
   for non-subscribers; New York requires registration and a bond above $5,000
   in prizes ([NY DOS](https://dos.ny.gov/games-chance-registration)); other
   states were not researched.
3. **Members act on picks in their own accounts.** Each member bets and
   withdraws themselves. Prefer sportsbook-authorised integrations (Action
   Network's [BetSync](https://www.actionnetwork.com/education/betsync)
   [secondary]) over collecting member logins (Pikkit's model,
   [Pikkit](https://pikkit.com/booksync) [secondary]); never hold funds, place
   bets or take write access; make no promise of winnings.
4. **Affiliate partnerships.** Revenue share usually pays on member losses,
   which conflicts with selling picks meant to help members win (our view, not
   a source). Every state's affiliate licensing and advertising rules apply
   (including under-21 audiences and responsible-gambling notices); only New
   Jersey, Pennsylvania and New York were looked at, and not fully. It would
   end the "no affiliate links" position in
   [LEGAL_COMPLIANCE_RESEARCH.md](LEGAL_COMPLIANCE_RESEARCH.md).

## 5. Questions for a gaming and securities lawyer, in order

1. Has LINEHOUND already taken any customer money, placed any bet for anyone, or
   promoted a fund, managed account or waitlist? Raise this first.
2. Which states are LINEHOUND and its customers in (including Nevada's messenger
   betting law and states without legal sports betting, not researched)?
3. Is there any licensed path to betting for customers? Check A.R.S. 13-3305
   and 13-3303, the Michigan, Colorado and Ohio rules, and DOJ's Wire Act
   position.
4. Is a real-money pool off the table under securities (federal and state),
   bookmaking, money-transmission and commodity-pool rules, and does the Kalshi
   litigation change that?
5. Does the picks service itself face state gambling-information statutes, and
   does the Wire Act safe harbour in 1084(b) apply?
6. Do per-sport pricing and record displays keep us an information service, and
   which FTC substantiation, endorsement, ROSCA and state automatic-renewal
   rules apply?
7. Can a free paper leaderboard offer prizes, and which entry, registration and
   bond rules apply by state?
8. Affiliates: flat fee or revenue share, which licences and advertising codes,
   and does it change our classification?
9. Money-laundering, forfeiture and federal wagering-tax exposure of anything
   above.
