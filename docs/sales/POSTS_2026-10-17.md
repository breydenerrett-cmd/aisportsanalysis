```
1/ We paused our MLB picks. 178 graded bets since Sept 22: 97-81, down 1.4 units. Roughly break-even minus the books' cut, and still a loss. Here is what we found when we took it apart, including the parts that do not flatter us.
```

```
2/ Finding 1: we paid extra on every bet, by design. Our rules only let a bet through when the price was worse than the market's own fair price. That cost about 2.4 points of probability per bet.
```

```
3/ Finding 2: our forecasts carried nothing the market lacked. Across 6,818 prop prices, ours were slightly worse than the books'. The best weight to give our opinion was zero, or a little below.
```

```
4/ Finding 3: our ranking picked our biggest mistakes. It ranked bets by how far our number sat from the books'. The biggest gaps were where we were most wrong. Our props averaged 61%. They hit 52%. The market said 52.5%.
```

```
5/ We also found a bug: total bases overs skipped a correction that hits overs got, so they looked 2 to 3.5 points better than they were. Fixing it would not have put us ahead of the market.
```

```
6/ Where we got lucky: at the market's own numbers, bets like ours were expected to lose about 7.6 units. We lost 1.4. That is a good run, not a skill.
```

```
7/ Now: picks are paused. The brief, the price board and the graded record continue. Picks return only if a test registered in advance shows we beat the closing price. We do not claim the briefs make money. Analysis, not advice.
```
