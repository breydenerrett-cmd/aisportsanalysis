## 0. What this would register, and what it does not claim

It would register two forward, paired comparisons, each asking one question:

> At the moment a bettor would decide, does a frozen candidate's probability
> carry information that the de-vigged market probability for the same contract
> does not, and is that information large enough to survive the margin the book
> charges?

Family A: MLB **batter total bases** (candidate exists). Family B: MLB **pitcher
strikeouts** (candidate does not exist; this arm cannot register until one is
built and frozen, section 15 D2).

It claims no edge. The owner's hypothesis, that props are a better modelling
opportunity than moneylines, is **a claim about probability quality relative to
price**. A more predictable outcome is not a better market: the book may price it
better too. So the primary metric is the paired Brier difference against the
market's own de-vigged probability, not the candidate's accuracy, hit rate or ROI.

The prior this registration starts from, so that a null is expected rather than
surprising: the paired Brier differences already in the matrix are batter total
bases -0.0069 (se 0.0083, n=46) and -0.0022 (se 0.0089, n=64), batter hits +0.0132
and +0.0075, all within noise; the median two-sided margin is 7.0% (total bases)
and 6.5% (strikeouts); no market is a CANDIDATE. Zero survivors is a valid result.

## 2. Hypotheses

For each family F in {A, B}, with one contract per player-game (section 3):

- **H_F (improvement, the only member of the multiplicity family):** mean over
  scored contracts of `d = Brier_candidate - Brier_market` is **less than 0**.
- **Harm check (reported, not in the family):** mean `d` greater than 0, tested
  one-sided at the same level. It decides the "worse than the market" label
  (section 11).

`Brier_x = (p_x - y)^2`, `p_x` the probability of the **Over** and `y` = 1 if the
stat cleared the line. Brier on the Over equals Brier on the Under, so there is no
side to choose and no selection on side.

## 3. Population and exclusion rules

**Regular season only** for the confirmatory read. Postseason games are their own
row (the project's calendar rule: `docs/PERFORMANCE_MATRIX.md`, "postseason entries
are their own rows"), descriptive, underpowered by design (section 9), never pooled.

**The contract and the unit.** The unit is one player-game: (event, player). Where
a book lists more than one line for the same player, the contract is the single
line whose de-vigged consensus Over probability at the decision board is closest to
0.5 (ties: the lower line). That choice uses only the market at the decision board,
never an outcome, and it removes the dependence of alternate lines on one another.
The inventory counts 15.7 such player-games per game for total bases and 1.9 for
strikeouts, with two or more two-sided books.

**Eligible** (all must hold at the decision board, none may use an outcome):

1. The contract has at least **2 two-sided books** (the baseline needs a
   consensus). The six-book floor of `src/analysis/prices.MIN_BOOKS` is reported as
   a sensitivity subset (A: about 3.6 per game, B: about 1.3 per game), not the
   primary population, because the inventory shows it excludes 80% (A) and 45% (B)
   of eligible lines (and every batter-hits line).
2. The line is a half-integer (a whole-number line can push; excluded and counted).
3. A book counts as two-sided only if both prices are present and the raw booksum
   is at least 0.98 (`src/analysis/derivative_prices.MIN_TWO_WAY_BOOKSUM`).
4. The decision row meets the staleness rule of section 4.
5. The game is a regular-season game; not postponed or suspended at first pitch.
6. Family A: the batter is in the posted lineup with a batting slot. Family B: the
   pitcher is the announced probable starter.

**Not an exclusion, and counted:** a scored contract whose player has no box row
grades VOID (never guessed; `src/board/settle_props.py`) and stays in the record
with its reason; an excluded-by-rule contract is logged with the rule that excluded
it. Exclusions that depend on the outcome (a pitcher who left after one batter, a
batter who pinch-hit) are **not** permitted: they stay in and are graded as the box
score grades them. Family B has one OPEN point here: the box store has no starter
flag, so a scratched starter who relieves would grade as a start; D3 requires a
demonstrated starter test before any grade.

## 4. Baseline (the market) and decision time

**Baseline probability** `p_market`: the mean over two-sided books of each book's
proportional de-vig Over probability (`src.core.odds.devig_two_way`, default
method), from the contract's **newest shared observation instant** on the decision
board, exactly the rule `src/analysis/prices.snapshot` and `derivative_prices`
already use. Sensitivities, reported and never decisive: power and Shin de-vig;
the six-book subset; the best-price-only quote.

**Decision board**, fixed in advance:

- Family A: the newest board observed at least 30 and at most 120 minutes before
  first pitch with the lineup posted (the existing gate pass, `src/pipeline/batter_props.py`,
  whose median lead is 101 minutes). A game with no such board has no decision and
  is logged as `NO_BOARD`.
- Family B: the T-90m board of `src/pipeline/prop_prices.py`. The T-30m board is
  the closing board (section 8), not the decision board, so the decision price is
  strictly earlier than the close.

**Stale or missing inputs.** A decision row must be written within 15 minutes of
its board's observation instant and before first pitch; otherwise the contract is
not scored (`STALE_BOARD`). (15 minutes is a draft constant, set from the hourly
capture cadence, not from any outcome; the owner may change it before
registration.)

**Decision row.** At the decision board, before first pitch, the pipeline appends
one row per eligible contract to an append-only ledger (new file, section 13):
contract id, the board's observation instant, `p_market`, the books that built it,
the candidate's probability and its workings, the candidate's code fingerprint and
parameter-file hash, or the abstention reason. **The ledger row is what is graded,
never a recomputation after the fact.**

**Input classes and abstention** (reusing the vocabulary of

**How abstentions are counted.** Every abstention is a ledger row with a reason

abstentions by reason, with the abstention rate. The paired comparison is over
scored contracts only. Because abstaining can select, the result also reports the
market probability distribution of abstained against scored contracts and the
paired difference restricted to contracts that the candidate would have scored under
a no-abstention rule where one exists. An abstention rate above 40% for a family
is reported at the top of its result as a limit on what it can say.

## 6. Primary metric

**Mean paired Brier difference `d`** over scored contracts (candidate minus market;
negative means the candidate is better, the sign the matrix uses). Standard error
clustered **by game** (two or more contracts in one game share park, weather,
umpire and pace), with a **slate-date block bootstrap** (2,000 draws, seed fixed at
registration) as the check; the verdict uses the larger of the two standard errors.
Interval: one-sided, at the level section 10 sets.

Log-loss difference is a secondary with the same clustering (a probability a
candidate pushes to 0.02 is punished harder there than in Brier).

## 7. Secondary metrics (none decides)

1. **Calibration by decile**: candidate and market probabilities in ten bins (equal
   count), observed Over rate and count per bin; logistic calibration intercept and
   slope with clustered intervals, for each of candidate and market.
2. **Forecast error.** Family B: mean absolute error of the candidate's expected
   strikeouts against actual, beside the market line used as a point forecast.
   Family A: the Brier and log-loss above (the line is the only point on the
   market's distribution).
3. **Closing-line value with the measured closing board** (section 8), in
   probability points of the de-vigged consensus, and in price terms at the average
   price, reported separately because price-based CLV is biased negative by about
   half the hold (`docs/PERFORMANCE_MATRIX.md`).
4. **ROI at the price taken**, reported at the average price across the books that
   quoted the side at the decision board (the "picks not prices" convention) and,
   separately, at the best available price labelled price improvement. Never the
   decision metric; never described as edge.
5. The same paired comparison on the six-book subset, under power and Shin de-vig,
   and on each calendar month, descriptive only.

## 8. Closing-line value: what has to be captured

Closing board: for each contract, a board observed **0 to 90 minutes before first
pitch**, with **at least 6 two-sided books**. A contract without one has CLV
`NOT_MEASURABLE` (never zero, never imputed), counted by reason.

- Family B already captures it (T-30m board, 83.9% of captured games, 55.2% of
  lines with six or more two-sided books).
- Family A does not: the gate board's median lead is 101 minutes and 35.1% of games
  are inside 90. It needs **one extra snapshot of `batter_total_bases` alone per
  game, 30 to 90 minutes before first pitch** (D4). Only about one line in five
  (19.9%) has six or more two-sided books in the `us` region, so CLV covers roughly
  3.6 player-games per game even with the snapshot. Whether region `us2` raises that
  is untested (a 2-credit probe; D5).

## 9. Minimum sample and the stopping rule

One confirmatory read per family, at a fixed sample, no early look and no
continuation (the SLOT_PROP rule):

| | Family A (total bases) | Family B (strikeouts) |
|---|---|---|
| Scored contracts | at least 900 | at least 900 |
| Distinct games | at least 100 | at least 100 |
| Distinct regular-season slate dates | at least 30 | at least 30 |
| Contracts with a measured closing board (for CLV) | at least 300, else CLV is reported NOT_MEASURABLE and the verdict cannot be above PROMISING | same |

The read happens when **all** floors of a family are met. If the 2027 regular
season ends first, the read waits for the next regular season with the same
floors. Floors are not lowered because the season was short.

**Planning arithmetic (assumptions, not measurements, none from a counted outcome).**
Per-contract standard deviation of `d`: about 0.07, from the existing matrix rows
(se x sqrt(n) = 0.077, 0.068, 0.056, 0.071 for the four prop rows; 0.053 and 0.058
for the two moneyline rows). Design effect for game clustering assumed 1.5. At
n=900 the standard error is 0.07 x sqrt(1.5/900) = 0.0029. The smallest true
improvement detectable with 80% power at one-sided 0.05 is (1.645 + 0.842) x 0.0029
= 0.0071. **A true improvement of 0.004 would be detected only about 40% of the time.**
This design can confirm a large advantage or show there is none that large; a small
real advantage reads NO EVIDENCE, by design. The interim planning figures are
replaced by the real clustered standard error at the read.

**Weeks to the floors.** Using measured volume (per game: 15.7 total-bases and 1.9
strikeout player-games with two or more two-sided books), measured capture coverage
(75% and 62% of slate games) and an assumed 85% non-abstention:

- **Total bases:** about 150 scored contracts per regular-season day at 15 games, so
  900 contracts in about 6 days; the **30-date floor binds: about 5 weeks into the
  2027 regular season** (opening day is not in the repo: UNKNOWN).
- **Strikeouts:** about 15 scored contracts a day at today's coverage, so **about 60
  days, roughly 9 weeks**; about 39 days (6 weeks) if capture coverage is held near
  95%, which means preventing a repeat of the 2026-09-28 to 09-30 credit-floor
  starvation (D6).
- **Postseason 2026 (separate row, descriptive):** at most 53 games by format, 10
  already dated 2026-09-29 to 10-01, so at most about 43 more; at most about 570
  total-bases and 70 strikeout contracts after abstention. Below every floor. It
  can be published as an underpowered descriptive row and decides nothing.
- **Nothing here can reach a verdict before 2026-10-31.**

## 10. Multiplicity

The confirmatory family is **{H_A, H_B}**: two hypotheses, one test each. Procedure:
Benjamini-Hochberg at q = 0.10 (the registry's `alpha_declared` convention) over
the two one-sided p-values of H_A and H_B, computed once at the two reads. If only
one family reaches its floors, its p-value is judged at q = 0.10 alone, and the other
family's later read is judged at the level the pair would have required, so a late
second read cannot be used to relax the first.

As a stricter check, reported beside it: Bonferroni at 0.05 / 2 = 0.025. A
**PROMISING** or better label requires passing BH at q = 0.10; a **WORTH BUILDING
AROUND** label requires passing the Bonferroni check as well. The secondary
metrics are exploratory: ten deciles per family, three de-vig variants, monthly
slices and the CLV and ROI numbers carry no claim and are not corrected because
they are not tested. The registry's 47 registered hypotheses (92 rows, 2026-10-01)
are not pooled in (`docs/ALPHA_REGISTRY_DESIGN.md`: a pooled q across families
with different outcome definitions would be false precision); they are recorded as
searched-so-far prior spend beside this family.

## 11. What counts as each result

Fixed now, applied identically to both families, per family:

- **WORSE THAN THE MARKET.** The harm check rejects at the section 10 level (the
  one-sided lower bound of `d` is above 0, with the same clustered standard
  error). Published first and prominently; the candidate is retired for the
  product, not retuned.
- **NO EVIDENCE.** Neither the improvement test nor the harm check rejects. This is
  the expected result and it is reported as a result, with the achieved standard
  error and the detectable effect.
- **PROMISING, UNCONFIRMED.** H_F passes BH at q = 0.10 with point estimate `d` of
  -0.004 or lower, but CLV is NOT_MEASURABLE (fewer than 300 closing boards) or the
  Bonferroni check fails. Not a product claim.
- **WORTH BUILDING A PRODUCT AROUND.** All of: (a) H_F passes BH at q = 0.10 and the
  Bonferroni check at 0.025; (b) point estimate `d` of **-0.004 or lower**, a
  difference worth more than the instrument's noise floor; (c) mean CLV in
  de-vigged probability points is positive with a 95% clustered lower bound above 0
  on at least 300 contracts with a measured closing board (the project's primary
  criterion, `docs/VALIDATION_CRITERIA.md`); (d) the candidate's calibration slope
  has an interval that includes 1 and no decile is more than 5 points from
  its observed rate with at least 50 contracts; (e) the falsification battery
  (section 12) passes; (f) the abstention rate is 40% or below. Even then the
  statement is "the candidate's probabilities carried information relative to the
  de-vigged market at the decision board", **not** "a bettor profits": the
  margin (6.5% to 7.0%) means a Brier gain is not a return, ROI is reported
  separately, and any customer wording needs its own decision.

## 12. Falsification battery (before any label above PROMISING)

Pre-declared; each must come out as stated or the label is capped:

1. **Instrument null**: split the books at random into two halves; the paired
   difference between half-A consensus and half-B consensus must be within 1.96
   clustered standard errors of 0 (the instrument has no built-in bias).
2. **Shuffle placebo**: permute candidate probabilities within slate date 1,000
   times; the real `d` must be below the 5th percentile and the shuffled mean `d`
   must be positive (a scrambled candidate is worse than the market).
3. **Positive control (Family A)**: the same candidate with the batting slot
   replaced by the league-average slot must be **worse** than the slot-aware
   candidate on the same scored contracts (the slot effect on plate appearances is
   certain; an instrument that cannot see it cannot see anything). A failed control
   makes every row UNDETERMINED, not NO EVIDENCE.
4. **De-vig sign check**: the sign of `d` is the same under proportional, power and
   Shin de-vig.
5. **Leakage audit**: a sample of 50 ledger rows per family re-derived from the
   stores as they stood at the decision instant; any input newer than the decision
   instant voids the family's read.

## 16. What this draft does not do

It does not register anything, change a rule, gate or threshold, fit or score a
model, compute a win rate or ROI on any new slice, or open `evidence/`, the results
csv or any sealed-window row. It does not say props are or are not better than
moneylines; at the sample this design can reach, the likeliest honest answer is
"no evidence either way" for a small difference, and the draft says so in advance.
